AIRPORT GEOTHERMAL SIMULATOR - SOURCES AND CALCULATIONS
=========================================================
Purpose: let you fact-check every number the simulator shows, by hand.
Every formula below is the one in js/engine.js (function names in brackets).
Worked numbers are the simulator defaults: State College PA, 500,000 sq ft,
100% heating/cooling demand, no incentive. Reproduce them with:
    node verification/dump.js        (prints the engine's numbers)
    python3 verification/verify.py   (independent recomputation, 21 checks)
    node tests/engine.test.js        (19 unit tests)

HOW TO READ THE CONFIDENCE TAGS
  [SOURCED]    value taken from a named public source (check the source).
  [PHYSICS]    textbook thermodynamics / unit conversion, no judgment.
  [CALIBRATED] chosen so the model reproduces a real project (Section 8).
  [ASSUMED]    engineering judgment. NOT proven. Listed in Section 10 so your
               team can replace it with a site or vendor number.
Honest summary: the MATH is verified exactly (Section 9). The INPUT numbers are
a mix of sourced, calibrated and assumed values. A pre-design simulator cannot
"prove" site costs; it can only be transparent. Section 10 lists what to confirm.

2. DEMAND MODEL  [degreeHours, buildLoads, loadsAt]
---------------------------------------------------
Monthly mean temperature Tm and a within-month spread sigma. Hourly temperature
in the month is treated as Normal(Tm, sigma). Expected degree-hours above a
balance temperature b (cooling) or below it (heating), per hour, are closed form:
    z = (Tm - b)/sigma
    E[(T-b)+] = sigma * ( phi(z) + z * Phi(z) )     phi, Phi = normal pdf, cdf
    heating uses the mirror form with z = (b - Tm)/sigma.
Multiply by hours in the month (24 x days).
Building loads (BTU/h):
    Heat  = UAh * sqft * (Tbh - T)+          UAh = 0.70 BTU/h-sqft-F, Tbh = 55 F
    Cool  = UAc * sqft * (T - Tbc)+ * (1+latent)   UAc = 0.70, Tbc = 60 F
    Peak loads use the design temperatures (S6) times the sizing factor 1.15.
Worked example, State College:
    peak heating = 0.70 x 500,000 x (55 - 5) x 1.15 = 20,125,000 BTU/h = 1,677 tons
    peak cooling = 0.70 x 500,000 x 1.15 (latent) x (88 - 60) x 1.15 = 12,960,500 BTU/h
                   (1,080 tons)
    (sizing factor 1.15 = design margin.)
    annual heating = 33.10 billion BTU = 331,000 therms of delivered heat
    annual cooling = 11.08 billion BTU = 923,000 ton-hours
The brute-force check (Python integrates the normal curve numerically) matches
the closed form to better than 0.00001%.
UA = 0.70 is [CALIBRATED] (Section 8); the textbook range for a large
commercial envelope plus ventilation is roughly 0.3-1.0.


3. TRADITIONAL (BASELINE) SYSTEM  [copBaselineCool, effInvCop, baseline in computeAll]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Heating: gas boiler, efficiency 0.85.
    gas therms = annual heating BTU / 0.85 / 100,000
               = 33,096,783,180 / 0.85 / 100,000 = 389,374 therms
Cooling: air-cooled chiller. COP is Carnot fraction:
    COP_air(T) = etaAir * (T_chw + 459.67) / ((T + airApproach) - T_chw)
    etaAir 0.33, chilled water 44 F, approach 20 F, clamped to 2..5
Energy is integrated over the temperature distribution, not computed at one
average temperature, because 1/COP is not linear in T:
    kWh = sum over hours of load(T) / COP(T) / 3412.14
    State College result: 978,515 kWh
Baseline operating cost:
    gas        389,374 x $1.07  = $416,630
    electric   978,515 x $0.14  = $136,992
    O&M        2.5% of plant capex = $78,789
    total                         $632,412 / yr
Baseline plant capex = cooling tons x $1,800 + heating MBH x $60
    = 1,080.04 x 1,800 + 20,125 x 60 = $1,944,090 + $1,207,500 = $3,151,575.
(A bug where a single conditional-mean COP understated this by 7.6% was found
by the independent check and fixed; it now agrees to 0.000%.)


4. OPEN-LOOP SYSTEM  [copHeat, copCool, open section of computeAll]
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Heat pump COP (Carnot fraction), temperatures in Rankine for the ratio:
    COP_heat = etaH * T_supply_R / (T_supply - T_source)     etaH 0.50, supply 110 F
    COP_cool = etaC * T_chw_R / (T_cond - T_chw)             etaC 0.45, chw 44 F
    T_source = groundwater 50 F - 3 F HX approach - dT/2 (5 F) = 42 F
    COP_heat = 0.50 x (110+459.67) / (110-42) = 4.19
    T_cond (cooling) = 50 + 3 + 5 + 8 F condenser approach = 66 F
    COP_cool = 0.45 x (44+459.67)/(66-44) = 10.3, clamped to the model max 6.00
Flow needed for a load Q (BTU/h) and water temperature change dT = 10 F:
    heating: gpm = Q x (1 - 1/COP) / (500 x dT)   (only the extracted heat comes from water)
    cooling: gpm = Q x (1 + 1/COP) / (500 x dT)   (load plus compressor heat is rejected)
    State College: heating 3,064 gpm at full peak; required (80% sizing) = 2,451 gpm
Aquifer limit: the climate preset says how much water the aquifer can supply
(State College 2,200 gpm). Design flow = min(required, supply) = 2,200 gpm.
Wells = 2 x ceil(flow / yield per well) = 2 x ceil(2,200/500) = 10 wells
    (production + injection pairs).
Coverage: sizing to a fraction c of peak load covers 1-(1-c)^2 of annual energy
if the load-duration curve is linear (the rest is met by the boiler/chiller):
    heating coverage 92.0%, cooling coverage 92.6%
Energy:
    heat pump heating kWh = annual heating x coverage / 3412.14 / COP = 2,131,500
    heat pump cooling kWh = 500,925
    pumping kWh = 160,869  (pump kW per 100 gpm x flow, scaled by (part-load)^1.5)
    total electricity 2,865,962 kWh;  remaining gas 30,966 therms
Cost (all x location cost multiplier, default 1.0):
    source = 10 wells x $100,000 + 2,200 gpm x $350 + $150,000 = $1,920,000
    heat pump plant = covered capacity (tons) x $2,000/ton = $2,408,271
    gross install = $4,328,271
    credit for avoided conventional plant (toggle, default on) = $2,281,263
    net install = $2,047,008
Operating cost:
    electricity 2,865,962 x 0.14 + gas 30,966 x 1.07 + O&M
    O&M = 1.5% plant + 3.5% of source = $103,324   ->  $537,692 / yr
Savings and payback:
    savings = 632,412 - 537,692 = $94,719 / yr
    simple payback = net install / savings = 2,047,008 / 94,719 = 21.6 years
    (undiscounted, no escalation, no demand charges)


5. CLOSED-LOOP SYSTEM  [closed section of computeAll, borefield solver]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Ground resistance (borehole thermal resistance form):
    R_total = g / (2 pi k) + 0.12          (0.12 h-ft-F/BTU = grout, pipe, fluid)
    g = 1.6 + 0.25 ln(N) (20/spacing)^1.2   N = number of boreholes
Heat per foot of bore from the fluid temperature range:
    q' = (T_ground - EFT_min) / R_total       (heating, EFT min 30 F)
    q' = (EFT_max - T_ground) / R_total       (cooling, EFT max 90 F)
Long-term ground drift when heating and cooling are unbalanced:
    drift F = 0.06 x (1.3/k) x imbalance (kBTU/ft/yr), clamped +-15 F
The sizing loop adds boreholes until the monthly loop temperature stays inside
the EFT limits for the 25-yr drift. Land = N x spacing^2 / 43,560 acres.
Cost = N x (depth x $15/ft + $1,500) + $40,000 fixed.
Results (State College default): see Section 7 table.
Closed-loop is [ASSUMED] to a larger degree than open-loop: g-function and
drift are simplified fits, NOT a full g-function (Eskilson) solution. Use
GLHEPro / EED for final design.


6. HYBRID SYSTEM  [hybrid section]
----------------------------------
Open share s% of the load is served by the open-loop subsystem, (100-s)% by the
closed loop. Each is sized for its own share and each subsystem's cost is added
once; no load is counted twice, and thermal (BTU) and electrical (kWh)
quantities are never mixed. Plus a fixed integration cost ($120,000). Slider
is not auto-favored: change it and the numbers change in either direction.

Climate data used (NOAA 1991-2020 normals, monthly mean F, Jan..Dec):
  Minneapolis   16.2 20.6 33.3 47.1 59.5 69.7 74.3 71.8 63.5 49.5 34.8 22.0
  State College 26.3 27.5 36.3 47.9 59.0 67.2 71.2 69.2 62.6 51.5 40.4 30.0
  Miami         68.6 70.7 73.1 76.7 80.1 82.8 84.1 84.2 83.0 80.1 74.8 71.2
  Phoenix       56.8 59.9 66.3 73.2 82.0 91.4 95.5 94.4 89.2 77.4 65.1 55.8
Other climate fields: sigma 9/8/5/11 F; ground = groundwater temp 49/50/79/78 F
(annual mean + ~2 F); aquifer supply 3000/2200/3500/1800 gpm and k
1.3/1.4/1.5/1.2 are [ASSUMED] (no public data at a generic site).

8. VALIDATION AGAINST A REAL PROJECT (Louisville SDF, S8)
---------------------------------------------------------
Inputs: 412,766 sq ft, Louisville monthly normals, k 1.4, sizing 100%, 25 ft spacing.
Quantity               Model             Published
Boreholes (500 ft)     549               648  (k=1.0 gives 723; bracket OK)
Land footprint         7.9 acres         about 9 acres
Energy reduction       72% (site)        about 40%
Annual savings         $209k             about $400k
Gross cost             $7.6M             $22M total (scope far larger)
Calibration decisions: UA set to 0.70, spacing 25 ft and default
land availability 20 acres, so the borefield size and land fall in the right
range. Findings to be honest about: the model's energy reduction is HIGHER than
published (different baseline, no heat recovery), and savings are LOWER (no
demand charge, different tariff). The cost scope in the published $22M includes
mechanical/electrical upgrades the simulator does not model. So Louisville
validates borefield SIZE and LAND, not cost or savings.
Reproduce: node verification/louisville.js


9. INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION OF THE MATH
---------------------------------------
verification/verify.py re-implements demand (numerical integration), baseline,
open-loop COP, flow, wells, energy, capex, opex, savings and payback in Python
without importing engine code, using the engine's default inputs. 21 of 21
checks agree within 0.00001% (the one numerically integrated value agrees to
0.0003%). The closed-loop g-function drift and hybrid are checked by unit
tests only (tests/engine.test.js: energy balance, no double counting, slider sums
to 100%, monotonic behavior), NOT by an independent recomputation.


10. UNVERIFIED ASSUMPTIONS - PLEASE CHECK BEFORE CITING
-------------------------------------------------------
 1. Cost benchmarks (well $100k, $350/gpm HX, $150k fixed, $15/ft bore,
    $1,500/bore, $2,000/ton heat pump plant, $1,800/ton chiller, $60/MBH
    boiler): get a driller / contractor / RSMeans quote.
 2. Carnot-fraction efficiencies (0.50, 0.45, 0.33) and supply/chilled-water
    temps: check manufacturer data for a water-source heat pump (e.g. AHRI 13256-1).
 3. Aquifer yield, supply and well yield (500 gpm): need a hydrogeologic study.
 4. UA 0.70, balance temps 55/60 F, latent factors, sigma: calibrated/rule of
    thumb; check with an energy model (EnergyPlus/eQUEST) for the real terminal.
 5. Closed-loop resistance and drift fits (g, 0.06 factor, 0.12 R_bore).
 6. Linear load-duration assumption behind coverage = 1-(1-c)^2.
 7. O&M percentages (1.5%, 3.5%, 0.3%, 2.5%).
 8. Prices S1/S2 are national averages, not your airport's tariff (no demand
    charges, no escalation, no discounting, undiscounted simple payback).
 9. Local cost multiplier defaults to 1.0.
 10. Ground/groundwater temperature = annual mean air + ~2 F.
 11. Regulatory: open-loop discharge/injection permits and water chemistry
    (fouling, corrosion) are not modeled.
 12. No hourly simulation; seasons use monthly normal distributions.

LIMITATIONS: pre-design comparison tool; not a substitute for GLHEPro/EED,
EnergyPlus, or a hydrogeologic and cost study.
